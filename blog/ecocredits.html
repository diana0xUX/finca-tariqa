Can a limestone hillside earn carbon credits?

What Regen Network ecocredits are, how they work, and what our 4.5 hectares could realistically generate — now, and when the EU market opens in 2028.

Ecocredits By Diana Sage August 2026 10 min read

Someone told us our land might be worth more than the oranges it grows — not because of what grows above the soil, but because of what is happening underneath it. Soil carbon. Holm oak roots. Mycorrhizal networks rebuilding after decades of synthetic inputs. The idea that a blockchain network could put a verified number on that, and that someone in Berlin might pay for it — seemed far-fetched. We looked into it anyway.

Here is what we found.

What is Regen Network?

Regen Network is a blockchain built specifically for ecological assets. It runs on Cosmos — the same infrastructure family as many Web3 projects — but instead of trading financial tokens, its purpose is to issue and track verified environmental credits. Think of it as a public ledger for ecological health.

There are three layers to understand:

Regen Ledger is the blockchain itself — a permanent, public record of which land is registered, which methodology was used, what monitoring data was submitted, and when credits were issued. Once recorded, it cannot be altered. This is the verification layer.

Regen Registry is the standard-setting body. It approves methodologies, reviews project applications, and commissions third-party verification audits. It functions like an organic certification body — except for ecological impact rather than farming inputs.

Regen Marketplace (app.regen.network) is where buyers and sellers meet. Buyers are typically companies seeking verified carbon removal for sustainability reporting. Sellers are land stewards who have earned credits through verified practices. As of mid-2026, the marketplace has recorded $2.7 million in cumulative volume — real, but thin. There are currently 26 active sell orders.

One ecocredit = one verified tonne of CO₂ equivalent removed or avoided. The REGEN token is something else entirely — a governance token for the protocol. Do not confuse them.

How credits are earned

Ecocredits are not purchased, mined, or generated by holding a token. They are earned by land, verified by science, and issued once. The process takes between 18 months and three years from start to first credit.

What Finca Tariqa could generate

Our site has two things that matter for soil carbon sequestration: deep-rooted trees transitioning away from intensive management, and limestone substrate that holds organic matter exceptionally well once it accumulates. The right methodology for us is the AEI Regenerative SOC Methodology v2.0, published May 2025. It covers agricultural lands under active regenerative management — exactly our situation.

At steady state — after three or more years of documented regenerative practice — land like ours typically sequesters between 2 and 4 tonnes of CO₂ equivalent per hectare per year. On 4.5 hectares, that is 9 to 18 credits annually. The holm oak reforestation adds a separate biodiversity layer that may qualify for a different credit class.

We have also established a soil carbon baseline in 2026 with an IVIA-accredited laboratory and registered under the Verra VCS VM0042 standard. This means we enter any future verification process with documented starting conditions — not a blank page.

The honest answer for today: not much. $45 to $270 a year is meaningful as a proof of concept, not as income. It does not replace what an orange harvest earns. But that is not really the point — at least not yet.

The point is documentation. Every soil sample taken now, every cover crop record filed, every year of verified organic practice accumulates into a baseline. When the EU Carbon Removal Certification Framework opens — expected around 2028 — farms that arrive with five years of documented improvement will be positioned to earn real revenue. Farms that start in 2028 will spend the next two years building the baseline they should have built now.

We are not doing this for the $45. We are doing it because the farm that documents 2026–2028 correctly will have a significant advantage when the EU market opens.

The EU Carbon Removal Certification Framework

The EU CRCF (Regulation EU 2024/3012) is the most significant carbon policy development for European farmers in a decade. It establishes a legally standardised framework for certifying carbon removal from agricultural and forestry land — with verification rules, additionality requirements, and a path to EU-recognised credits.

The framework is expected to be operational around 2028. Credits certified under it would be eligible for EU sustainability reporting compliance — which means corporate buyers will pay significantly more for them than for voluntary market credits. Projections based on EU ETS price trajectories suggest €60–100 per tonne.

There is no confirmed partnership between Regen Network and the EU CRCF yet. But the Verra VCS standard we are already registered under is one of the reference methodologies likely to be recognised. We are keeping both paths open.

The roadmap for Finca Tariqa

2026: Soil carbon baseline established (IVIA-accredited lab). Verra VCS VM0042 registration active. Organic conversion process begun under CAECV. Holm oak reforestation in Year 1.

2027: First full monitoring year under regenerative management with documentation. Begin Regen Registry project application. Await CAECV certification outcome.

2028: Third-party verification audit (if project plan approved). First ecocredits potentially minted. Monitor EU CRCF opening and compatibility with existing certifications.

2029–2030: Annual credit cycles. Expand forest carbon accounting as holm oak establishes. Evaluate biodiversity credit eligibility as understorey recovers.

What we are not doing

We looked at Toucan Protocol, which once allowed tokenising Verra credits on Ethereum. Its original developers have since abandoned the project. The credits it issued ("BCT" and "NCT" tokens) are widely considered low-quality and are no longer accepted by serious buyers. We are not using it.

We are also not treating carbon credits as a primary revenue stream. They are a byproduct of doing the land management right — organic inputs, holm oak reforestation, cover crops, no tillage. If the carbon market rewards that in 2028, we will be ready. If it does not, the land will be healthier regardless.

· · ·

The conversation about money and land has been changing. A hillside that sequesters carbon, hosts biodiversity, and grows food under certified organic conditions has more potential value streams than any of its predecessors did. We are learning, carefully, how to speak all those languages at once — without letting any of them replace the actual work of farming.

The oranges still matter most. But now we know what is happening in the soil beneath them, and that knowledge has value too.
