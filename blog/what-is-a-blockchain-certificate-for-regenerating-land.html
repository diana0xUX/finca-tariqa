What is a blockchain certificate for regenerating land?

Diana Yakupova  ·  Carcaixent, Valencia

Alisher has returned from Argentina in the end of 2025 wehere he spent a month in a camp building the future alongside enthusiasts and visionerees. there he met farmers who are reshaping the future. One of the ideas was "tokenize our ecological impact." The land is worth more than we think — not because of the oranges, but because of what the soil is doing. 

We spent weeks researching it. Here is what we found...

First: there are three completely different things

When people say "blockchain certificate for land," they usually mean one of three very different things. They have different buyers, different laws, and different economics. Mixing them up is an expensive mistake.

Infographic — The Three Types

Not all land certificates are the same thing

🌿
Carbon Credit
You get paid for carbon your land removes from the atmosphere. 1 credit = 1 verified tonne of CO₂. Think of it like getting paid for a service — the service of pulling carbon out of the air.
Who buys it: companies trying to offset their emissions — airlines, food brands, manufacturers.
Relevant for us ✓
🦎
Biodiversity Credit
You get paid for measurable improvement in wildlife, habitat, and ecosystem health on your land. A newer idea, with no dominant European standard yet. The market is thin and fragmented in 2026.
Who buys it: companies with nature-positive pledges, or developers legally required to offset habitat damage.
Emerging for us ✓
🏦
Land Ownership Token
Fractional ownership of the land itself, sold as a digital token — like selling shares in your farm. Has nothing to do with ecological impact. It's a financial investment product, regulated under EU law (MiCA).
Who buys it: investors betting on the land's value going up, the way you'd buy stock in a company.
Different conversation

For Finca Tariqa: carbon credits and biodiversity credits are the relevant ecological certificates. Land ownership tokens are a separate fundraising structure — legally regulated as financial instruments under EU MiCA regulation (December 2024).

What this means: Carbon credits are not income. At our farm size, €11–252 per year is meaningful as a proof of concept, not as revenue. The reason to register now is documentation — each year we record our practices becomes evidence worth money when the EU carbon market opens in 2028, where prices are projected at €60–80+ per tonne. The farm that documents 2026–2028 correctly enters that market with years of verified improvement. A farm that starts in 2028 starts with nothing.
We are not doing this for €11 a year. We are doing it because the documentation we build in 2026 is what makes 2028 worth something.

What our farm can actually do — right now

Most blockchain land certificate programs are built for large landholdings in the Global South, or for arable crops like wheat and rapeseed. A 4.5 hectare limestone hillside in Valencia with citrus and native reforestation doesn't fit neatly into most of them.

Here is what does fit, in order of accessibility:

Infographic — Platform Ladder for Finca Tariqa

Four realistic paths, ranked by how easy they are to start

1
MITECO — Spain's government registry
Free national registry for CO₂ absorption projects, run by Spain's Ministry for Ecological Transition (Royal Decree 214/2025). Register the holm oak reforestation zone (where no forest existed before). Spanish companies are now legally required to calculate and offset their carbon footprint — MITECO credits are the only ones that qualify for the official Spanish seal. A real and growing domestic buyer market.
Free
2
Open Forest Protocol — blockchain, no upfront cost
A blockchain registry (runs on NEAR blockchain) specifically for reforestation and agroforestry. Minimum project size: 0.3 hectares — our reforestation zone easily qualifies. Submit ground monitoring every 6 months for the first two years, then annually. Credits are issued on-chain and sold to international buyers. A limestone hillside with native holm oak in Valencia is a genuinely compelling story to northern European corporate buyers.
Free
3
Regen Network — whole farm, fees unknown
The most philosophically aligned system. Built specifically for regenerative land. Can issue credits for carbon, biodiversity, and soil health together — not just one thing. Accepts our whole farm: citrus terraces and the reforestation zone. No stated minimum size. The catch: they don't publish their fees publicly. Requires a direct email to ask. Timeline: 18 months to first credit minimum.
Email first
4
EU CRCF — the big opportunity, not yet open
The EU Carbon Removal Certification Framework (Regulation 2024/3012) passed December 2024. The methodology covering agroforestry on mineral soils was adopted July 2026. A full EU registry opens December 2028. When it does, credits certified under CRCF will command premium prices — because EU corporate sustainability laws will require them. The documentation we build now is what makes us ready to enter that market on day one.
From 2028

Why we register now even though the money is small

The EU Emissions Trading System (compliance carbon for large industries) trades at around €84 per tonne in 2026. The voluntary market — where farms and forests sell credits — trades at €7–35 per tonne. That gap exists because voluntary credits lack the legal weight that CRCF certification will create.

When the EU CRCF market opens around 2028, projections based on EU ETS price trajectories suggest €60–80+ per tonne for certified agricultural carbon. That would put our farm in the range of €270–720 per year — still supplemental, but real. And farms entering the market in 2028 with five years of documented regenerative practice get priority access and better prices than farms starting from scratch.

Six-monthly ground monitoring reports for Open Forest Protocol. CAECV organic certification process underway. First credits may be issued from OFP — small amounts, but verified and on-chain. MITECO registration active.

A second soil carbon measurement creates the first before/after data point. This is what carbon credit auditors verify: not just that you're managing well, but that things are improving. Two measurements are worth ten times one.

CRCF-certified credits command €60–80+/tonne because they satisfy EU corporate sustainability law. A farm entering in 2028 with documented improvement since 2026 is a credible seller. A farm starting in 2028 with no prior documentation is not.

What not to do

The research turned up a number of paths that look appealing but aren't. These are the ones to avoid.

Infographic — What Doesn't Work for Our Farm

Four dead ends

✕
Agreena
One of the largest soil carbon programs in Europe, operating in Spain. Sounds perfect. But their eligibility rules explicitly exclude citrus, olive groves, and any woody perennial crops. They only accept arable land growing wheat, barley, rapeseed, or legumes. Our citrus grove and native reforestation are both excluded by definition.
✕
Verra or Gold Standard — on our own
These are the two largest voluntary carbon registries globally. Credible, well-known, with premium buyers. But the verification audit alone costs €15,000–25,000 — a fixed cost that doesn't scale down for small farms. At our income of €11–252/year from carbon, it would take decades to break even. The only path to Verra is through an aggregator (like Agreena), which doesn't accept our land type.
✕
KlimaDAO / Toucan / BCT tokens
These were the DeFi carbon projects of 2021–2022. They tokenized Verra credits onto Ethereum and Polygon and promised liquid carbon markets. By 2025, Toucan handed the protocol to the community and pivoted to AI tools. KlimaDAO relaunched at far smaller scale on Base blockchain in February 2026 — daily trading volume is now around €150. Effectively illiquid. Not a viable path for any new project.
✕
Treating carbon credits as primary income
Even in the most optimistic scenario — OFP + MITECO + CRCF by 2028 — total carbon revenue for our farm might reach €500–1,500/year at scale. The oranges earn more. The ecotourism will earn more. Carbon documentation is a credibility layer, a premium buyer story, and a bet on the 2028 market. It is not a financial foundation on its own.

The three actions that cost nothing

All the research points to the same conclusion: the most valuable thing we can do in 2026 is document. The system that will reward that documentation (EU CRCF) isn't open yet. But the documentation window is open now, and it won't wait.

Three emails and one soil test. That's the whole action plan this year.

Email MITECO — or call a Spanish environmental consultancy (CO₂ Revolution, eco2for) to ask how to register the reforestation zone as an official absorption project under RD 214/2025. Free to register, free to maintain.

Apply to Open Forest Protocol — at openforestprotocol.org. The 0.3 ha minimum means our reforestation zone qualifies. No upfront cost. Ground monitoring twice a year for two years, then annually. Credits issued on NEAR blockchain.

Email Regen Network — registry@regen.network — and ask what it costs to register a 4.5 ha agroforestry project in Spain. They don't publish fees publicly. We need to know before we can decide.

And the soil sample — taken by an IVIA-accredited lab this year — is the baseline that everything else is measured against. Without it, we have nothing to verify in 2028.

· · ·

The conversation about land and value is changing. A hillside that sequesters carbon, protects biodiversity, and grows food under organic certification has more potential revenue streams than any farm in this valley did twenty years ago. We are learning, carefully, how to speak all of those languages at once — without letting any of them replace the actual work of farming.

The oranges still matter most. But we know what's happening in the soil underneath them, and that knowledge is starting to have a price.
