Priority 1 · Highest impact
Organic certification + tree health
€1,800

The most important question this year is understanding why production has dropped and what it means for the grove's long-term health. That answer drives everything — the organic conversion strategy, the cooperative relationship, and what we can honestly promise to adopters about future harvests.

We're applying for organic certification through the CAECV (Comité de Agricultura Ecológica de la Comunitat Valenciana) under EU Reg. 2018/848. If the previous owner can confirm no prohibited inputs in recent years, we may qualify for retroactive recognition — potentially skipping most of the standard 3-year conversion wait. We're pursuing this actively. Separately, Exagro — the cooperative currently handling the grove — has indicated they will accept transition-period oranges, which changes the financial picture significantly: the conversion period becomes revenue-generating, not revenue-neutral.

CAECV registration fee — fixed €247.68 + variable €64.04 (2.81 ha citrus × €22.79/ha) €312
Retroactive conversion application — includes required soil and tissue lab analysis to document prior clean management. €357
Agronomist consultation — expert assessment of the production drop. Foliar and soil analysis. Written diagnosis and management recommendations. €800
Administrative and travel — CAECV office (Carlet), cooperative meetings, inspector visit. €331
Subtotal €1,800

Annual maintenance fee (~€281/yr) is eligible for 80% GVA subsidy once registered — effective annual cost ~€56 thereafter.

Priority 2 · Legal and practical
Farmhouse legalisation + water filtration
€2,700

The farmhouse is now habitable — windows, doors, floors, walls, bathrooms, and a kitchen in progress. Water and electricity are connected. What remains is making it official: obtaining the cédula de habitabilidad (habitability certificate) from the Generalitat Valenciana, and the licencia de primera ocupación from the Ajuntament de Carcaixent. Without these, the building cannot legally be used for guest stays, which is the eco-tourism revenue stream planned from Year 2.

Separately, the connected water supply needs a household filtration system — standard practice for rural agricultural properties where the supply runs through older infrastructure.

Architect's habitability certificate — licensed architect inspects and signs off the completed works, issues cédula de habitabilidad documentation for GVA submission. €1,200
Licencia de primera ocupación — municipal application fee to the Ajuntament de Carcaixent. Confirms building was completed per original licence. €400
GVA registration fees — Conselleria de Habitació administrative costs for the cédula de habitabilitat. €300
Household water filtration system — multi-stage unit (sediment + activated carbon + UV) suited to rural agricultural supply. Supply and installation. €800
Subtotal €2,700

Exact administrative fees to be confirmed with local architect. Figures above are estimates based on standard GVA and Ajuntament rates for comparable rural properties.

Priority 3 · Future value
Soil carbon baseline + monitoring equipment
€2,000

The EU Carbon Removal Certification Framework opens around 2028. A grove with documented soil carbon records from 2026 has two years of verified improvement to show when that market opens. We need two things: a certified lab baseline now, and our own instruments to collect continuous data ourselves — the kind of ongoing, granular monitoring that backs digital land recovery certificates and makes the data credible year-round, not just at annual inspection time.

IVIA-accredited lab baseline — 5 soil cores across the grove, organic carbon %, bulk density, pH, texture. Two depth intervals. Formatted for Agreena / Verra VCS VM0042 registry submission. €450
Soil moisture and temperature sensors — 4–6 units, buried at root depth across terrace zones. Continuous data logging. €600
Compact weather station — on-site microclimate: temperature, humidity, rainfall, solar radiation. Needed for carbon methodology calculations. €450
Data logger + connectivity — low-power hub to collect and transmit sensor readings. Cloud storage integration for digital certificate data pipeline. €350
Setup, calibration and first data review — on-site technician commissioning, baseline readings documented. €150
Subtotal €2,000

Priority 4 · Ongoing
Terrace recovery — DANA storm damage
€2,500

In October 2024 the DANA storm dropped 500 mm of rain in eight hours across the Valencia region. The valley below us flooded catastrophically. The limestone hillside shed the water through its fractures and recharged the aquifer — the geology held. The lower terraces did take some damage: debris, downed branches, terrace wall repairs needed, and trees that will benefit from proper post-stress pruning before this season's fruit matures. This is not emergency work — the trees are producing — but it needs doing before the end of the season.

Agricultural labour — experienced team of 2 for 3 weeks. Debris clearing, stress pruning, terrace walk and assessment. €1,800
Equipment hire — tractor and trailer for debris removal, chipper for branch waste into mulch. €400
Terrace wall repair materials — stone and lime mortar for minor structural repairs to lower terrace edges. €300
Subtotal €2,500

What this campaign does not cover

This campaign is purely operational — physical and legal work on the land. It does not fund website hosting, our own time, administration of the adoption programme, or any marketing spend. Those are covered by tree adoption subscriptions. The two pots are separate, with separate purposes.

Every euro donated goes into the farm — into the ground, the paperwork, or the instruments to read what the ground is doing.
