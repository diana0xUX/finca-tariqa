The Forest

Replacing fire risk with deep roots.

1.7 hectares of Franco-era Aleppo pine are being cleared and replanted with Quercus ilex — the native holm oak of the western Mediterranean limestone. A 15-year transition that changes the ecology of the hillside.

Why

The wrong tree, for 70 years.

Aleppo pine was planted across Valencia's hillsides in the 1950s and 60s under Franco's reforestation programme, primarily to stop erosion. It worked. The trees are now mature, highly flammable, and ecologically foreign to this substrate.

Quercus ilex — holm oak, alzina — is the native climax species for this elevation, aspect, and limestone substrate. It lives for 700 years. It builds soil instead of exhausting it. It does not burn the way Aleppo pine does. And it is beginning to regenerate naturally in the gaps where the pine canopy opens.

The transition — 2026 to 2040+

Mature holm oak woodland.

Self-sustaining. Carbon-certified. The landscape this hillside was always building toward.

The Carbon Connection

The baseline starts now. The market opens in 2028.

The EU Carbon Removal Certification Framework (Regulation 2024/3012) becomes operational approximately 2028. Agricultural producers with established soil carbon baselines and documented management records will have verified credits to sell. A grove that starts the baseline now enters that market with years of data. One that waits starts with nothing.
