What we're working on

Four things. This year.

Not a five-year vision. Not a pitch deck. These are the four actual things happening on this hillside right now, and what this campaign makes possible.

🌪️
Recovering from DANA
The October 2024 DANA storm left fallen trees across the grove and the pine forest. Clearing the damage is the foundation — before planting, before certifying, before hosting anyone. We're removing deadfall, stabilising the terraces, and assessing what was lost. This is Year 1 priority one.
🌿
Going organic
We've mapped the full CAECV certification process, identified the application forms, and sent inquiries to the previous owner and the cooperative about treatment history. If the grove has been clean for three years, the conversion clock may already be done. We're filing this year. Read the full story →
🔗
Ecocredit certification
We're registering the farm on the Regen Network — the blockchain standard for verified ecological impact. 1 credit = 1 tonne of CO₂ verified. The soil carbon baseline we take this year becomes the starting point for credits when the EU carbon market opens around 2028. The documentation starts now. How it works →
🏡
Opening the doors
The farmhouse exists. It needs a habitation certificate and some care before it can legally host overnight guests. We want to create a space for people who care about land, food, and regenerative thinking — farmers, researchers, makers — to come and stay on the hillside. A community, not a hotel.
Year 0 — Already done

Before we ask, here's what we've built.

The campaign starts from a running start, not a blank page. Every item below is complete or actively in motion before a single euro of campaign funding arrives.

November 2025
Farm acquired
4.51 hectares of Upper Cretaceous limestone hillside in Carcaixent, Valencia — purchased for €95,000. 1,100 citrus trees across three varieties (Clemenules, Navelina, Navel Lane-Late), average age 50 years, active cooperative membership, functioning drip irrigation, a farmhouse, and 1.7 ha of pine forest above the grove. The land has income from day one.
✓
October 2024 – June 2025
First harvest delivered under new ownership
14,209 kg of Navel Lane-Late oranges harvested and delivered to the cooperative. First full season managed under current ownership. Income confirmed. The cooperative relationship is active and operational.

Water tested
Two well water samples analysed by accredited laboratory. Result: safe for irrigation — pH 7.4, conductivity and salts within citrus tolerance, Legionella not detected. The well is confirmed functional.

How the money is used

Year 1. Six priorities.

One year of focused work. Costs sourced from IVIA, CAECV, and Valencia-based suppliers. Nothing estimated loosely. Year 2 planning begins when Year 1 is funded.

Year 1 — Foundation
November 2026 – October 2027
€10,000
DANA cleanup & land clearing Storm damage from October 2024 left fallen trees and deadfall across the grove and pine forest. Clearing this is the precondition for everything else — planting holm oak, running soil samples, hosting visitors. Manual labour on terraced limestone: no machinery can reach the upper grove. Includes debris removal and terrace inspection. €3,000
Farmhouse — habitation certificate Architect inspection and cédula de habitabilidad — the legal prerequisite for overnight guests. Without it, the farmhouse cannot host anyone. This is the single enabling step for the community space. Includes any minor works identified by the inspection, basic outdoor area setup. €2,000
CAECV organic certification Registration fee plus retroactive conversion recognition application (Form FPO05-157e2). If the grove has had no synthetic inputs for 3+ years, the three-year conversion wait may be waived. Filing this year means organic pricing possible in 2027–2028. Includes first-year inspection fee. €700
Ecocredit registration & soil baseline Regen Network project registration plus five soil core samples (0–30 cm and 30–100 cm), IVIA-accredited lab analysis for total organic carbon, bulk density, pH. This baseline, taken before any management changes, is what makes future carbon credit claims verifiable. Without it, we cannot enter the EU CRCF market when it opens around 2028. €800
Holm oak — Phase 1 500 containerised Quercus ilex seedlings plus individual tree shelters, planted into the first cleared zone of the pine forest in November. The native species this limestone hillside wants. Replaces fire-prone Aleppo pine with a species that lives 700 years and self-seeds once established. €2,500
Cover crops, admin & professional fees Hairy vetch and grass mix across the citrus terraces (first organic practice, compatible with current certification, immediate soil biology benefit). Plus agricultor activo registration with Conselleria d'Agricultura (prerequisite for Joven Agricultor €70K grant), PTGFS forest management plan drafting, leaf mineral analysis. €1,000
