The Fruit

Stressed to perfection.

On limestone hillside, mild chronic water stress does to citrus what Burgundy does to wine — concentrates sugars, spikes aromatics, and makes fruit that tastes of the place it grew.

The Science

Why limestone changes everything.

♡

Mineral profile

Cretaceous limestone, dissolved slowly over 80 million years, feeds the trees minerals that valley alluvial soil cannot replicate.

○

Natural deficit

Thin soil, fast drainage, Mediterranean sun — the tree experiences mild water stress every summer. Brix (sugar concentration) rises. Valley fruit cannot match it. The same mechanism that winemakers in Burgundy and Priorat deliberately engineer — here, the geology provides it for free.

◆

Terpene spike

Under stress, citrus redirects energy into secondary aromatic compounds — limonene, aromatic aldehydes. The chemistry of perfume. Water-deficit citrus carries measurably deeper aromatics than irrigated valley fruit.

Three Varieties

October to June. Without a gap.

Oct – Dec

Clemenules

The mandarin the world wants, right when it peaks.

The grove's most abundant variety. In strong seasons, over half grades CAT1 — the top commercial classification. Biennial rhythm: record harvest one year, quieter the next. On this hillside, the Brix is consistently higher than valley floor fruit — perceptible to any taster who eats both side by side.

Apr – Jun

Lane-Late Navel

The late one. Worth waiting for.

Arrives in April, alone in a market with fewer competitors. Five consecutive seasons of growing volume. Agronomic research on warming Mediterranean climates identifies late navels as a strategic variety — their harvest window shifts later, into space that earlier navels cannot fill. This hillside is positioned for it now.

Dec – Mar

Navelina

The classic. In recovery.

300 trees on limestone, actively managed through diagnosis after the October 2024 DANA floods. When healthy — and these trees have been — they produce the most deeply flavoured fruit of the winter season. The diagnosis is underway. The second season is about acting on what was found.
